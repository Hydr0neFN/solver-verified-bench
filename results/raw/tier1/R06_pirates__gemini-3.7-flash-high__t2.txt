### Backward Induction Reasoning

- **Survival & utility preservation**: Proposers minimize mortality risk and allocate minimal surplus necessary to incentivize agreement.
- **Incentive constraint**: Bloodthirst implies a vote is `NO` unless the offer is strictly greater than the voter's counterfactual payoff if the proposer dies ($c_{\text{offer}} \ge c_{\text{next}} + 1$).
- **$N=5$ state**: P2 requires 3/5 votes. In $N=4$, P4 and P6 receive 0. P2 bribes P4 and P6 with 1 coin each $\rightarrow$ allocation $(98, 0, 1, 0, 1)$.
- **$N=6$ state**: P1 requires 3/6 votes (self + 2). In $N=5$, P3 and P5 receive 0. P1 secures P3 and P5 with 1 coin each, yielding net positive utility to both and guaranteeing survival for all.
- **Payoff vector**: P1 keeps $100 - 2 = 98$, giving 1 coin to P3 and 1 coin to P5.

FINAL: P1=98, P2=0, P3=1, P4=0, P5=1, P6=0
