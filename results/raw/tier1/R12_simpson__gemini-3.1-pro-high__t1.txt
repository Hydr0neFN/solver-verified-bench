This is a classic example of Simpson's Paradox. We can determine the best treatment by comparing their success rates for each specific condition (stone size), which controls for the confounding variable.

**Success rates by stone size:**
*   **Small stones:** Treatment A is **93.1%** (81/87) successful, while Treatment B is **86.7%** (234/270). Treatment A is better.
*   **Large stones:** Treatment A is **73.0%** (192/263) successful, while Treatment B is **68.8%** (55/80). Treatment A is better.

Because Treatment A yields a higher success rate for *both* small and large stones, a patient should prefer Treatment A regardless of their stone size. The overall aggregate rates are misleading because Treatment A was assigned disproportionately to the more difficult (large stone) cases, dragging its overall average down. 

**Treatment A's overall aggregate success rate:**
*   Total successes: 81 + 192 = 273
*   Total cases: 87 + 263 = 350
*   Rate: 273 / 350 = 0.78 = 78.00%

FINAL: prefer=A, A_overall=78.00%
