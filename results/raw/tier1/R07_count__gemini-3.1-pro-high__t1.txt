To find the number of 8-digit strings formed by the digits 1 through 9 with no two adjacent digits equal and a sum divisible by 3, we can use a symmetry argument.

First, let's find the total number of valid 8-digit strings without the divisibility condition. 
- The first digit can be any of the 9 non-zero digits (9 choices).
- Each subsequent digit must be different from the immediately preceding one (8 choices each).
Thus, the total number of valid strings is $9 \times 8^7$.

Now, consider the mapping $f$ that increments each digit by 1 modulo 3, specifically: 
- $1 \to 2, 2 \to 3, 3 \to 1$
- $4 \to 5, 5 \to 6, 6 \to 4$
- $7 \to 8, 8 \to 9, 9 \to 7$

Since $f$ is a bijection, $d_i \neq d_{i+1} \iff f(d_i) \neq f(d_{i+1})$. Therefore, applying $f$ to all digits of a valid string always yields another valid string. 

Because we apply this mapping to an 8-digit string, the sum of the new string's digits will be congruent to the original sum plus $8 \times 1 = 8 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$.
Applying $f$ a second time yields a sum congruent to the original sum plus $4 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$. 
Applying it a third time returns the string to itself.

This creates orbits of 3 strings each. Within each orbit, exactly one string has a sum $\equiv 0 \pmod 3$, one has a sum $\equiv 1 \pmod 3$, and one has a sum $\equiv 2 \pmod 3$.

Thus, exactly one-third of all valid 8-digit strings have a sum divisible by 3. 

The number of such strings is:
$\frac{1}{3} \times (9 \times 8^7) = 3 \times 8^7 = 3 \times 2,097,152 = 6,291,456$
